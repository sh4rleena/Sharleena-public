Classical massage

Complemented with cupping and foot reflexology, tailored to you.

In my massages I give your body impulses for relaxation, self-regulation and recovery. Your body carries within it the ability to find its way back into balance. The massage creates the space for this.

The foundation is classical massage. With targeted techniques I release tension in the muscles and connective tissue, stimulate circulation and bring your nervous system to rest.

Depending on your complaints and how you feel, I complement the treatment with cupping or with elements of foot reflexology. Before each treatment we take time for a short conversation so that I can respond to your wishes and needs.

Feeling instead of analysing. You can simply be here, breathe and feel.

Cupping

A traditional drawing-out method

Cupping is one of the so-called drawing-out methods. Cups are placed on the skin using negative pressure. The suction stimulates circulation in the tissue and can loosen muscles and connective tissue. According to traditional European understanding, cupping is also said to stimulate the flow of lymph and to support the body in its own cleansing processes.

I place the cups on specific areas or move them gently over the skin as a cupping massage, entirely according to your needs. Cupping combines well with classical massage.

The negative pressure can temporarily cause reddish to purple discolouration of the skin. This is generally harmless and usually fades within a few days.

Foot reflexology

A holistic approach through the feet

Foot reflexology is based on the idea that the whole body is reflected in the feet. Each organ and each region of the body is assigned to a particular zone on the foot. In this way, the whole body can be reached through the feet. According to traditional understanding, treating these zones can stimulate the body’s self-regulation, release tension and support inner balance.

With gentle, targeted pressure on the reflex zones, I address your body on a different level. I adapt the treatment to how you are feeling and give attention to areas that need particular care. Many people find foot reflexology deeply relaxing and experience a sense of calm and groundedness afterwards.

Individual zones may feel sensitive during the treatment. This is normal and often gives an indication of where in the body something is currently out of balance. I always adjust the intensity together with you.

Phytotherapy – Herbal medicine

Plants are among humanity’s oldest remedies. Phytotherapy, also known as herbal medicine, uses flowers, leaves, roots, bark and seeds. It gently supports your body and stimulates your self-healing powers.

My approach

My work with plants is strongly shaped by humoral medicine. This ancient theory of the bodily fluids is one of the foundations of Traditional European Naturopathy (TEN). It sees the person as a whole. Each of us has our own constitution, our own temperament.

Plants have a character too. They have a warming or cooling, moistening or drying effect. That is why I do not simply choose “a remedy for” a symptom. I look for plants that suit you and your current state and help you find your inner balance again.

How the plants reach you

Teas
The most original form of herbal medicine. Hot water is poured over dried plant parts, which release their active substances. A tea has a mild effect and is well suited to longer use. Even the preparation becomes a small ritual for yourself.
Tinctures
Here plants are steeped in alcohol. It extracts the active substances and keeps them for a long time. Just a few drops are enough, so tinctures are easy to fit into your daily life.
Spagyric blends
Spagyrics goes back to the alchemical tradition and to Paracelsus. The plant is fermented and distilled. The residues are reduced to ash, and the mineral salts obtained are returned to the distillate. In this way the whole plant with all its components is meant to be preserved.
Ready-made herbal medicines
These are tested and approved herbal medicinal products from the pharmacy or chemist. Their content of active substances is standardised and their dosage precisely defined.

We will decide together which form suits you best.

Humoral medicine

Humoral medicine, also known as the theory of the four humours, is one of Europe’s oldest healing concepts. It goes back to the Greek physician Hippocrates and was later developed further by the Roman physician Galen. It shaped European medicine until the 19th century.

According to this theory, four fluids are at work in the body: blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile. Each fluid has particular qualities and is warm or cold, moist or dry. When the fluids are in balance, we feel well. If this balance is disturbed, complaints can arise according to traditional understanding.

Which fluid predominates in a person shapes their constitution, their temperament:

Sanguine (blood)
warm and moist, cheerful and sociable
Choleric (yellow bile)
warm and dry, energetic and passionate
Melancholic (black bile)
cold and dry, profound and careful
Phlegmatic (phlegm)
cold and moist, calm and balanced

Plants have these qualities too. Ginger, for example, warms and dries, whereas mallow cools and moistens. This allows me to restore balance in a targeted way: someone who carries too much cold within them will tend to receive warming plants.

Today we no longer understand the four humours literally, but as a model for thinking. It helps to see the person as a whole and not just the individual symptom.

Naturopathic nutritional counselling

Ancient knowledge and new insights for a lasting change

What we eat accompanies us every day. A suitable diet can strengthen you and support your body in its natural processes. There is no single right diet for everyone.

In my nutritional counselling I combine two perspectives. The first comes from the humoral medicine of Traditional European Naturopathy (TEN). According to its understanding, foods also have particular qualities. They have a warming or cooling, moistening or drying effect. Spices, the way food is prepared and the season also play a role.

The second perspective comes from current nutritional science. It shows what nutrients such as protein, fats, vitamins and minerals do in the body and how meals affect blood sugar. It also shows how important a healthy gut is for overall well-being. This knowledge flows into my counselling just as much as tradition does.

At the beginning we take time for a detailed conversation. We look at your eating habits, your daily life, your complaints and your goals. From this we develop steps together that fit well into your life. It is not about strict rules or crash diets, but about a change you are happy to keep up.

Food that brings joy can nourish you on every level.
